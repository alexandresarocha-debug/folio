Eva Ranehill, Anna Dreber, Magnus Johannesson, Susanne Leiberg, Sunhae Sul, and Roberto Weber ran a larger version of the power-pose experiment, with the experimenter blind to condition. Two hundred people, 98 women and 102 men, gave saliva, then held two high-power or two low-power poses taken from the 2010 study while they did a filler task. A first calculation said 100 people would give an 80 percent chance of detecting an effect as large as the one Dana Carney, Amy Cuddy, and Andy Yap had reported. After looking at those first 100, the team ran 100 more. They put their power to detect an effect of that original size above 95 percent.

One piece of the original result came back. People who had held the expansive poses reported feeling more powerful. The rest did not. Testosterone did not rise with the open posture, and cortisol did not fall. In a gamble like the original, a sure amount of money against a 50-50 chance of more or nothing, pose made no reliable difference. It made none when the gamble was framed as a loss, and none when people chose whether to solve math problems under a competitive payment scheme or a piece rate. Those last two tasks were additions. Using only the single gain-domain choice that matched the 2010 gamble left the conclusion unchanged. Carney, Cuddy, and Yap had found their hormone and risk effects in 42 people. This study was built to see effects of that size, and it did not see them.

The split is the useful part for anyone about to coach a posture before an interview or a negotiation. An open stance can change the answer to "how powerful do you feel?" In this sample it did not change the hormones, and it did not change the decision to take a risk or to compete. A shift in a self-report is a small, ordinary finding, and it can also be a person telling the study what the pose seemed to ask for. It is a weak reason to expect a better financial choice. The 2010 claim that two minutes of posture prepares the body for power is the claim this experiment was powered to detect, and the hormones and the gambles stayed flat.
