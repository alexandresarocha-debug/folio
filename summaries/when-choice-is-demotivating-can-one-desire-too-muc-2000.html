Sheena Iyengar and Mark Lepper's 2000 paper has three studies. The first is the one everyone remembers. At an upscale grocery store, a tasting booth offered jams, and on different occasions it showed either 6 flavours or 24. Shoppers who stopped could taste and get a coupon toward a jar.

The large display drew a crowd. Of 242 customers who passed it, 60 percent (145) stopped. Of 260 who passed the small display, 40 percent (104) stopped. But the people at the large display did not taste more: they sampled about 1.5 jams on average, against 1.4 at the small one. And when it came to buying, the result flipped. Nearly 30 percent (31) of the people who stopped at the 6-jam booth bought a jar. Only 3 percent (4) of those at the 24-jam booth did. Note that these are shares of the people who stopped, not of everyone who walked by.

In the second study, students in an introductory college course could write an optional essay for extra credit. Some were given 6 possible topics and some 30. More students wrote the essay when they had 6 topics, 74 percent against 60 percent, and the essays from the smaller list were rated better.

In the third study, people chose a chocolate from a display of either 6 or 30 and tasted it. Those who chose from the 30 reported enjoying the choosing more, but found it more difficult and frustrating, and were more dissatisfied and regretful about what they picked. Afterwards they could take either money or chocolates as payment for taking part. Of those who had chosen from 6, 48 percent took chocolate. Of those who had chosen from 30, only 12 percent did.

What this means in real life: a big menu, a wall of products or a long list of options can feel good at first and still make it harder to act. Some people respond by deciding less, by putting off the choice, or by regretting the one they took.

Criticism: later studies did not always find it. The next two works in this section are the meta-analyses that tried to sort out when the effect occurs. The jam result in particular comes from one store.
