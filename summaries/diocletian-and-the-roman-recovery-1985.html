On 20 November 284, at Nicomedia, the army proclaimed a Dalmatian soldier emperor. His name was Diocles. He took the throne name Diocletian, and he had the praetorian prefect Aper killed. Stephen Williams's Diocletian and the Roman Recovery, first published in 1985, follows the reign that closed a half-century of short, violent reigns. The repair had to be military and political at the same time. In 286 Diocletian made Maximian a fellow Augustus. On 1 March 293 he added two junior emperors, the Caesars Constantius and Galerius, so that a ruler could stand near the Rhine, the Danube, Britain, and Persia in the same season. Constantius's forces took Britain back from the breakaway regime of Allectus in 296. The Persian war ended in a settlement favorable to Rome in 299.

The Edict on Maximum Prices, issued in 301, shows where a decree stopped. The government posted maximum prices and wages for well over a thousand goods and services. The penalty named in the edict was death. Pieces of the inscription survive across the eastern provinces, so the order was published very widely. Lactantius, writing in Nicomedia, described shops emptying and the edict failing. By the time Diocletian left office it was largely a dead letter. Williams gives the text an appendix of its own. A state that can carve a price list into stone all over the east is a state with reach. Reach is not the same thing as control of a market.

On 1 May 305 Diocletian resigned at Nicomedia, and Maximian resigned at Milan. They named successors and stepped down while they still ruled. The college had been built so that the man could be replaced. Within a few years the armies and the ambitions of the new rulers tore the roster apart, and the civil wars that followed produced Constantine. The quieter frontiers and the heavier administration lasted longer than the handover. Williams's narrative is a case of a state rebuilt by organization, and of a succession that worked on the day it was announced and failed once the organizer had gone to his palace at Split.
