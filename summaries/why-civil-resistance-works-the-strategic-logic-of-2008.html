Many political scientists assumed that armed struggle was the most effective way for opposition groups to win against powerful governments. Maria Stephan and Erica Chenoweth tested that assumption by building the Nonviolent and Violent Conflict Outcomes (NAVCO) data set, with aggregate data on 323 violent and nonviolent resistance campaigns from 1900 to 2006. They included campaigns for regime change, against foreign occupation, or for secession or self-determination, but not broad social movements such as the US civil rights movement.

Their main finding was that major nonviolent campaigns achieved success 53% of the time, compared with 26% for violent campaigns.

They offered two main reasons. First, a commitment to nonviolent methods strengthens a movement's domestic and international legitimacy and encourages broad-based participation, which puts more pressure on the regime. Second, violent repression of unarmed protesters is more likely to backfire, bringing sympathy and support at home and abroad. Members of the security forces are more likely to disobey orders or defect when told to use violence against unarmed demonstrators than against armed insurgents.

To test these mechanisms, they compared three cases in Southeast Asia where both violent and nonviolent resistance were used: the 'people power' movement that ousted Ferdinand Marcos in the Philippines in 1986, the failed uprising in Burma in 1988 to 1990, and East Timor's struggle against Indonesian occupation from 1988 to 1999.

What this means in real life: the authors expanded the work into the 2011 book Why Civil Resistance Works, and its central claim, that nonviolent resistance can be the more effective strategy and not only the more principled one, now shapes debates among scholars and activists.

Caution: the authors call their main limitation hard to avoid. Their data may be biased towards success, because large, mature campaigns are most often recorded, while would-be nonviolent campaigns crushed in their infancy never make it into the data. They tried to address this by asking experts to check for missing failed movements.
