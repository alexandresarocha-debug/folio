Roy Baumeister, Ellen Bratslavsky, Mark Muraven, and Dianne Tice brought 67 hungry undergraduates into a room that smelled of chocolate chip cookies baking in a small oven. Cookies and chocolate candies sat on the table beside a bowl of red and white radishes. The cover story was a taste test, and the students had been asked to skip a meal. People told to eat radishes had to leave the cookies alone. The experimenter watched through a one-way mirror. Nobody bit into the wrong food, though a few picked the cookies up to smell them. They then tried to trace geometric figures without lifting the pencil or retracing a line. The test figures could not be solved, and they could quit whenever they liked. Radish eaters quit after 8.35 minutes and 19.40 attempts. Students who had eaten cookies lasted 18.90 minutes and 34.29 attempts. Students given no food lasted 20.86 minutes and 32.81 attempts. Mood did not differ between the radish and chocolate groups. The cost was the resisting.

A second experiment replaced the cookies with a choice. Students who agreed to give a speech in favor of a tuition rise, a position most of them disliked, later gave up sooner on the same kind of puzzle than students who were simply assigned that speech. Students who freely chose to give a speech they already agreed with also quit sooner. Reported mood did not explain the gaps. The authors' reading was that choosing, resisting, and persisting draw on one limited strength. Spend it on the first demand and the next demand gets less of it.

The story is an easy one to take home: after a day of holding your temper, skip the hard decision, because the tank is low. The 1998 gaps are large, and the samples are small, a few dozen students in a single lab. They have not held up as a general fact. In 2016 a registered replication led by Martin Hagger, across 23 laboratories and 2,141 people, used a computer task that Baumeister had recommended and found an effect of d = 0.04, a confidence interval that included zero. Feeling spent after a hard stretch is a familiar experience. A shared inner fuel that runs out in the way the radish experiment claimed is something the later, larger tests did not find.
