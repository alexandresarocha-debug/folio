Vladimir Propp sat down with 100 fairy tales from Alexander Afanasyev's Russian collection and asked what stayed the same when the names changed. His answer, in Morphology of the Folktale (1928), is that the functions stay the same. A function is an action defined by what it does in the tale, not by who performs it. A dragon can kidnap the tsar's daughter, a witch can kidnap a boy, or older brothers can carry off the younger brother's bride. Propp files all three as one function, villainy: the villain causes harm to a member of a family. He marks that function as the point where the tale really starts to move. The earlier moves only prepare it. Someone leaves home. An order is given, and then broken. The villain tries to find something out, and succeeds. The villain deceives the victim, and the victim helps him. Then the harm is done, or else the hero discovers a lack, and the story of repair can begin.

He finds thirty-one such functions, and he claims the order does not reshuffle. Tales skip functions. They do not, on his account, rearrange the ones they use. The last of them is often a wedding, a reward, or the villain's punishment. He also sorts the actors into seven roles: the villain, the donor, the helper, the princess and her father, the dispatcher, the hero, and the false hero. The roles can be split or combined. One person can be both donor and helper. What matters is the slot, not the costume. A horse, an eagle, or a grateful dead man can all carry the hero to the other kingdom. That flight is one function, spatial transfer, whoever does the carrying.

The method is a good cure for the habit of treating every new villain as a new kind of story. If the harm, the departure, the gift, the struggle, and the return line up, you are looking at one tale-shape with the paint changed. The limit is the shelf he used. These are Russian wonder tales, not myths, not novels, and not every folktale. In 1960 Claude Lévi-Strauss argued, in "Structure and Form," that a single running order taken from one body of fairy tales could not be the whole structure, because important relations cut across that line rather than sitting in it. Later tellers and editors also swap and repeat moves that Propp's sequence keeps in one direction. Count the functions when you want to see the skeleton of a fairy tale. Do not expect the same thirty-one steps, in the same order, once you leave that kind of tale.
