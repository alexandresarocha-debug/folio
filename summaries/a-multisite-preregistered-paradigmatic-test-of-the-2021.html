Kathleen Vohs, Brandon Schmeichel, and 36 laboratories preregistered a test of ego depletion with 3,531 people, mean age about 21. Each lab chose one of two opening tasks meant to burn self-control. Sixteen labs, 1,679 people, used a writing task. Twenty labs, 1,852 people, used a letter-e task in which the hard version required people to withhold a practiced response. Everyone then worked on figure-tracing puzzles of the kind used in the original depletion experiments. The preregistered score combined how long they kept going with how many sheets they tried. The rules for dropping people were fixed in advance. They removed 1,068 participants, 30.25 percent of the sample, mostly for too many errors on the letter task, for language, or for using a phone.

The opening task did what it was supposed to do as a chore. People in the hard condition rated it more effortful and more difficult than people in the easy condition. The difference was large for the writing task, d = 3.09, and smaller for the letter task, d = 0.46. On the puzzles, the registered difference between hard and easy was d = 0.06, with a 95 percent confidence interval from −0.02 to 0.14. A Bayesian analysis that began from a moderate effect, around 0.30, found the data about four times more likely if the effect was absent. Putting the excluded people back in, an analysis the authors marked as exploratory, produced a small significant gap, d = 0.08. On time alone, the hard-condition group quit about 27 seconds sooner, 985 seconds against 1,012. That duration gap was not the test they had registered.

After a tedious stretch of self-control, a large collapse on the next hard task is the wrong thing to expect from these data. The laboratory story in which willpower is a fuel tank came from smaller studies. This project, like the 2016 registered replication across 23 labs, finds at most a sliver, and the sliver is not distinguishable from zero in the analysis that was locked first. Tiredness and a wish to stop are still ordinary facts of a long day. They are a different claim from a resource that 36 labs could not reliably show running out.
