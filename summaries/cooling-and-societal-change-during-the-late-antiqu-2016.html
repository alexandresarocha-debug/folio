Ulf Büntgen and his colleagues dated a cold spell with the rings of larches. They measured 660 trees at five high sites in the Russian Altai and Sayan mountains, 152 of them still living and 508 of them dead wood on the ground, and set that series beside an Alpine tree-ring record from about 7,600 kilometers away. The two curves move together. In the Altai reconstruction the 540s are the coldest decade of the last two millennia, 3.2 degrees Celsius below the 1961–1990 summer average. In the Alps the same decade is the second coldest, 1.9 degrees below that average. Of the twenty coldest Altai summers in the whole record, thirteen fall in the sixth century after 536.

The trigger they use is a cluster of eruptions already dated in polar ice. A northern eruption in March 536 carried an estimated global forcing of −11.3 watts per square meter. A tropical eruption in 540 reached −19.1, larger than Tambora in 1815, which they put at −17.2. A smaller eruption followed in 547, at −1.1. The cold outlasted the dust. Ocean and sea-ice feedbacks, plus a dip in solar output, kept summers down. They name the stretch from 536 to about 660 the Late Antique Little Ice Age, and they note that 65 temperature-sensitive records from the extra-tropical Northern Hemisphere agree on a widespread cool phase. They then place the cold next to the plague that reached the Mediterranean in 541, the strains on the eastern Roman and Sasanian empires, movements off the steppe and out of Arabia, the spread of Slavic-speaking peoples, and political crisis in China.

The rings are a thermometer with a calendar. A summer 3.2 degrees colder in the Altai, and nearly 2 degrees colder in the Alps, is enough to wreck a harvest, and it lines up with writers from Constantinople to Italy who described a sun without warmth. The further claim, that this cold helped establish the plague or pushed armies out of Arabia a century later, is a historical suggestion laid on top of the dates. The paper offers the cold as one more factor, not as a single cause. The part another researcher can check is the physical one: three dated eruptions, one exceptionally cold decade, and a cool phase the authors run on to about 660.
