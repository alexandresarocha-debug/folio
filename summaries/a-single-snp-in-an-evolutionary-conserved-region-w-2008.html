The 2004 twin study pointed to a region of chromosome 15 around the OCA2 gene. OCA2 helps make melanin, the brown pigment in the iris. Brown eyes have a lot of it. Blue eyes have very little. Richard Sturm and colleagues in Brisbane went looking for the exact DNA letter that matters.

Their sample came from Queensland adolescent twins, their brothers and sisters, and their parents. DNA was available for 3,839 people in 1,037 families. The team tested 92 more single-letter DNA differences (SNPs) in 300 to 3,000 people of European descent.

The winner was not inside OCA2 at all. It was a single letter, called rs12913832, inside the neighbouring gene HERC2, about 21,000 letters upstream of where OCA2 starts. On its own it explained about 68% of the variation in eye colour, with an evidence score (LOD) of 444. In their sample, 78% of copies carried the blue version, the letter C.

The odds were striking. People with two blue copies (C/C) had only a 1% chance of having brown eyes. People with two brown copies (T/T) had an 80% chance of brown eyes. So this one letter predicts blue against brown very well, but not perfectly.

Why does a letter in one gene change another? The letter sits in the middle of a short stretch of DNA that has barely changed across many species. The blue version breaks it up. The authors concluded that this stretch acts as a control switch for OCA2, and that the blue version turns OCA2 down in the iris, so less pigment is made.

A second letter fine-tunes the result. A change inside OCA2 called R419Q shifts the odds of green or hazel eyes. Among T/T people with two copies of the 419Q version, 50% had green or hazel eyes. With one copy it was 21%, and with none, 6%.

What this means in real life: one DNA letter is the main switch between blue and brown, and other genes add the shades in between.

Caution: the sample was of European descent. The scale had only three colours, and 20% of T/T people did not have brown eyes, so other genes clearly matter. The switch idea is the authors' reading of their evidence, not a direct experiment in this paper.
