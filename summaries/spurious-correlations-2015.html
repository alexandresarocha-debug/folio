Tyler Vigen's Spurious Correlations began as a website in 2014 and was published as a book in 2015. Each chart lays two unrelated yearly statistics on top of each other so that they appear to move in step. Vigen describes the project as fun first and mildly educational second, and says it is not meant as an attack on correlations or p-values, which are useful when used correctly. The charts are free to reuse under a Creative Commons licence, which is one reason they turn up in so many classrooms and textbooks.

In January 2024 he released a big update, adding 25,000 new variables and a tool for discovering new matches. One of the best-known pairs is the number of films Nicolas Cage appeared in each year and the number of drowning deaths in the United States. On the current site, for the 34 years from 1980 to 2013, the correlation is r = 0.559, so about 31% of the variation in drownings is statistically predictable from Cage's output. The p-value is 0.00059, which would normally be called highly significant.

The site then explains why that number misleads. Vigen deliberately dredges the data: if you correlated about 1,702 random variables over those years, you would expect to find one this strong by chance. Neighbouring years are not independent, because people do not change their habits on January 1, which makes a standard p-value look stronger than it should. Outliers matter, and with only 34 data points one odd year can move the result. He says he does not trim years to boost a correlation, but notes that someone sneakier could.

What this means in real life: when you see a striking chart linking two trends, ask how many other comparisons were tried and not shown, and whether a third factor, such as time or population, could drive both.

Caution: the charts are jokes by design, and their real lesson is about data dredging, not about any particular pair. The site's numbers are recalculated as its data change, so older versions of the same chart may show different figures.

Source. Vigen, T. (2015). Spurious Correlations. New York: Hachette Books. Website (2014, updated January 2024): https://www.tylervigen.com/spurious-correlations. Example chart: Nicolas Cage films and US drowning deaths.
