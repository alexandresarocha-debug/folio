Daniel Oppenheimer, then at Princeton, started with a survey. Of 110 Stanford undergraduates, 86.4% admitted they had made their vocabulary more complex in academic essays to sound more intelligent, and nearly two-thirds said they had used a thesaurus to do it. He then ran five experiments to see whether the strategy works.

In Experiment 1, 71 students read excerpts from personal statements for graduate study in English literature. A highly complex version replaced every noun, verb and adjective with its longest entry in the Microsoft Word 2000 thesaurus. Readers decided whether to admit the applicant. Complex texts were rated harder to read and were less likely to win acceptance, whatever the quality of the original essay.

Experiment 2 used two English translations of Descartes's fourth Meditation, of similar length, one judged considerably more complex than the other. When the passage was attributed to Descartes, the simpler translation earned an intelligence rating of 6.5 against 5.6 for the complex one. In Experiment 3, a sociology dissertation abstract was simplified by replacing long words with shorter synonyms; the simplified version's author was judged more intelligent, 4.80 against 4.26.

The last two experiments tested the mechanism. In Experiment 4, an essay printed in a hard-to-read italic font made its author seem less intelligent, which suggests that difficulty of processing, or low fluency, drives the effect. In Experiment 5, the essay was printed on a printer low in toner. With an obvious excuse for the difficulty, readers over-corrected and rated the author more intelligent than with a normal printout.

What this means in real life: in applications, reports and essays, plain words usually serve the writer better than impressive ones, and a clean, readable format matters too.

Caution: the samples were small groups of Stanford students, and only written texts were tested. Oppenheimer notes that a long word is sometimes the most precise choice, and the study does not show that sophisticated vocabulary is always harmful.
