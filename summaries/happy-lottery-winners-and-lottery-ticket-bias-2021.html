The world spends about $250 billion a year on lottery tickets, write Seonghoon Kim of Singapore Management University and Andrew Oswald of the University of Warwick, yet it has proved surprisingly hard to show that lottery wins make people happier. They call this the famous and still unresolved paradox due initially to Brickman and colleagues, and offer an explanation.

Their idea is lottery-ticket bias. The way to win more is to buy more tickets. If a researcher sees someone who won $1,000 but does not know they spent $1,000 on tickets to get it, the win will look as if it did nothing for them. Because most studies have no data on ticket spending, estimates of the happiness effect of wins are biased downwards.

To test this they persuaded the organisers of a regular survey in Singapore, a country with intense lottery playing, to add questions about how much people spent on lottery tickets. The sample was 5,626 people. Without any adjustment for ticket spending, they found the famous null result: lottery winnings were unrelated to life satisfaction. Once spending was included, lottery wins were linked to substantial improvements in happiness and well-being.

They also criticised the original study. It used cross-sectional data on only 22 winners and 22 comparison people, the winners were more likely to have no educational qualifications, 32% against 18%, and the authors did not adjust for such differences. Kim and Oswald suggest it would not have been easy to publish today.

What this means in real life: comparing winners with non-winners can mislead if winners have also spent more to win.

Caution: the data are cross-sectional and wins and spending are self-reported. The study shows a correction to an old method rather than settling how much a large win changes a life.

Source. Kim, S., and Oswald, A. J. (2021). Happy lottery winners and lottery-ticket bias. Review of Income and Wealth, 67(2), 317-333 (published online 22 May 2020). doi:10.1111/roiw.12469. Open-access copy: https://wrap.warwick.ac.uk/id/eprint/153706/
